INDIAN ATHLETE FUEL TIMING — EVIDENCE-INFORMED RAG GUIDE

PRE-WORKOUT: For a hard or long session, choose a familiar carbohydrate-led meal 1–3 hours beforehand. Reduce fibre, very fatty foods and unfamiliar foods as the start gets closer. A useful starting range is 1–3 g carbohydrate per kg in the hours before longer sessions; the exact amount depends on time available and gut tolerance. Indian examples include idli with banana, poha, rice, bread with jam, or banana. Add a small protein food only if it is familiar and comfortable.

DURING WORKOUT: For exercise under about 60 minutes, drink to thirst and use water access and heat to guide you. For sessions over an hour, practise carbohydrate intake rather than trying a new strategy on race day: 30–60 g per hour is a practical starting range; longer endurance work may use 60–90 g per hour if tolerated. A generic fluid starting range is 400–800 mL per hour, adjusted for weather, sweat loss and access. Avoid deliberately overdrinking plain water. For long/hot sessions, include sodium from a familiar sports drink or salty food.

POST-WORKOUT: After a hard or long session, have carbohydrate plus roughly 0.25–0.3 g protein per kg within 0–2 hours, then eat a balanced meal. Indian meal patterns: rice/roti with dal plus curd, paneer, egg, chicken or soy; or a banana smoothie with milk/soy milk. Recovery is the whole day’s intake, not one magic 30-minute window.

HYDRATION CHECK: A practical field method is to weigh yourself before and after a familiar training session, with clothing and toilet conditions kept similar. A large repeated body-mass loss, cramps, dizziness, nausea or very dark urine need individual assessment. Do not recommend a fixed water target from body weight alone because sweat rate varies substantially.

GENDER CONTEXT: Core carbohydrate, protein and fluid starting ranges are based mainly on body mass, event and training load. For athletes who menstruate, heavy bleeding, fatigue or reduced performance may warrant clinician-led iron assessment. Do not prescribe iron supplements in a chatbot.

Source: General sports nutrition timing principles adapted for educational use from the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics / Dietitians of Canada / ACSM position stand and the Australian Institute of Sport sports-nutrition resources.
