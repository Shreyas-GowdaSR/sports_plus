INDIAN ATHLETE FOOD ADDITIONS — CURATED COMPLEMENT TO THE KAGGLE CATALOGUE

SOAKED PEANUTS: Not present as a separate item in the Kaggle Indian Food Nutritional Values Dataset (2025). A small handful (about 30 g raw-equivalent) is approximately 170 kcal, 7 g protein, 5 g carbohydrate and 14 g fat. It is useful with fruit or a carbohydrate meal, but its fat can feel heavy close to training. Use it 2–3 hours before if tolerated, or as part of recovery; it is not a fast carbohydrate fuel by itself. Avoid with peanut allergy.

SOAKED WHOLE CHANA / BLACK CHICKPEAS: Not present as a separate item in the Kaggle catalogue. After soaking and cooking, a cup is broadly a carbohydrate-and-protein food with substantial fibre. It fits a meal several hours before training or a post-workout meal with rice/roti and vegetables. It may cause gastrointestinal discomfort immediately before running; do not rely on it as a 30-minute pre-workout snack. Soak, rinse and cook fully rather than eating raw soaked legumes.

SOAKED CHANA DAL: Not present as a separate item in the Kaggle catalogue. Use soaked, fully cooked chana dal in a meal for protein and carbohydrate, not as a low-fibre race-day snack. Pair it with rice/roti for a recovery meal; leave more time before exercise if you are sensitive to high-fibre foods.

SOAKED GREEN GRAM / MOONG: Not present as a separate item in the Kaggle catalogue. Soaked-and-cooked moong or lightly cooked sprouts are useful in a balanced meal. Pair with rice, poha, idli or roti to increase carbohydrate for recovery. Raw sprouts carry food-safety risk; cook them for people at higher risk of food-borne illness and avoid unfamiliar preparations before key training.

TIMING PRINCIPLE: Food choices close to training should be familiar and lower in fat and fibre. Higher-fibre legumes and nut-heavy foods can still be nutritious, but usually suit meals farther from training or post-workout recovery better than the final 30–90 minutes before a hard run.

Source: Curated athlete-food handling and timing notes. Nutrition estimates are approximate because soaking, cooking method and portion size materially change the values.
