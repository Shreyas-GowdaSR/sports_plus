## Principles of gait retraining
Source: Barton CJ et al. Running retraining to treat lower limb injuries: a mixed-methods study of current evidence synthesised with expert opinion. Br J Sports Med 2016;50:513-526
Link: https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2015-095278

Experts in this review agree that form changes should be simple and practised a little at a time. Change one thing at a time with one simple cue ("quicker steps", "run quietly", "knees forward"). Use feedback (a metronome, a mirror on a treadmill, or video) at first, then slowly take it away so the new pattern becomes automatic. Start with a few minutes inside easy runs and build up over 2-6 weeks. Expect some new muscle soreness, but stop if you get sharp pain.

## Drills for quicker steps and a better landing
Source: Barton CJ et al. Running retraining to treat lower limb injuries: a mixed-methods study of current evidence synthesised with expert opinion. Br J Sports Med 2016;50:513-526
Link: https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2015-095278

Common coaching drills to practise landing under the body with a quick rhythm (based on coaching practice, before easy runs or after a warm-up):
- Metronome runs: 4-6 x 2 minutes on an easy run at 5-7% above your normal step rate.
- Strides: 4-6 x 80-100 m, relaxed and fast, walking back to recover, focusing on quick light steps.
- A-march and A-skip: 2-3 x 20 m, knee up, foot landing under the hip.
- Quick feet (fast small steps on the spot or over 10 m): 3 x 10 seconds.
- "Run quietly" cue: on an easy run, try to make your foot strikes as quiet as possible for 1-minute periods; quieter landings usually mean softer loading.

## Drills for posture, arms and hips
Source: Barton CJ et al. Running retraining to treat lower limb injuries: a mixed-methods study of current evidence synthesised with expert opinion. Br J Sports Med 2016;50:513-526
Link: https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2015-095278

Coaching drills to practise good posture and control (based on coaching practice):
- Wall lean drill: lean against a wall with a straight body from ankles to head, then march, to feel the forward lean coming from the ankles. 3 x 20 seconds.
- Arm swing drill: sit or stand and swing the arms from the shoulders, elbows at 90 degrees, hands brushing the hips, not crossing the middle. 3 x 30 seconds.
- Single-leg balance with a level pelvis in front of a mirror: 3 x 30-45 seconds each side.
- Mirror running on a treadmill: watch that the pelvis stays level and the knees point forward, a few minutes at a time.
- Wide-step running cue: run as if each foot lands on its own line, for 1-minute periods, if the knees fall inward.

## Calf and foot strength for runners
Source: Blagrove RC, Howatson G, Hayes PR. Effects of strength training on the physiological determinants of middle- and long-distance running performance: a systematic review. Sports Med 2018;48(5):1117-1149
Link: https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-017-0835-7

The calf muscles and Achilles tendon take very high loads with every running step. Strong calves help running economy and protect the shins and Achilles. Simple exercises 2-3 times a week: double-leg calf raises building to single-leg calf raises (3 sets of 15-20 each side, slowly), bent-knee calf raises for the deeper calf muscle, skipping or pogo hops (3 x 20 seconds) once strength is good, and toe yoga or towel scrunches for the foot muscles.
