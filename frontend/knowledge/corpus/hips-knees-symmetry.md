## Pelvic drop and hip control
Source: Noehren B, Hamill J, Davis I. Prospective evidence for a hip etiology in patellofemoral pain. Med Sci Sports Exerc 2013;45(6):1120-1124
Link: https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0b013e31828249d2

When one hip drops while the other leg is in the air (pelvic drop), the thigh of the standing leg moves inward (hip adduction). In this study, female runners who later developed runner's knee (patellofemoral pain) had more hip adduction during running before they got injured. An earlier study by the same group linked the same pattern with iliotibial band (ITB) syndrome on the outer knee. In the app's form check this appears as "Pelvic level" or "Pelvic stability". Weak hip muscles on the side of the body (gluteus medius) are a common reason.

## Knee falling inward
Source: Barton CJ et al. Running retraining to treat lower limb injuries: a mixed-methods study of current evidence synthesised with expert opinion. Br J Sports Med 2016;50:513-526
Link: https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2015-095278

When seen from the front, the knee should stay roughly in line with the hip and the second toe while the foot is on the ground. A knee that falls inward (dynamic knee valgus) often goes together with pelvic drop and is a pattern experts target in runners with knee pain. Useful cues: "knees pointing forward", "run with the feet slightly wider apart, as if on two lines", and "squeeze the buttocks a little when you land". In the app's form check this appears as "Knee & foot tracking".

## Strength alone may not change running form
Source: Willy RW, Davis IS. The effect of a hip-strengthening program on mechanics during running and during a single-leg squat. J Orthop Sports Phys Ther 2011;41(9):625-632
Link: https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2011.3470

In this study, six weeks of hip strengthening made the hip muscles stronger and improved single-leg squat control, but did not change how the runners moved during running. A later study by the same group found that gait retraining with a mirror, where runners practised keeping the pelvis level and the knee in line, did reduce hip adduction and knee pain, and the change lasted. So strength work is useful, but to change running form you also need to practise the new movement while running.

## Hip strength exercises for runners
Source: Willy RW, Davis IS. The effect of a hip-strengthening program on mechanics during running and during a single-leg squat. J Orthop Sports Phys Ther 2011;41(9):625-632
Link: https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2011.3470

Simple exercises that target the side and back of the hip, done 2-3 times a week: side-lying leg raises (3 sets of 12-15 each side), clamshells with a band, side plank (3 x 20-40 seconds each side), glute bridges and single-leg bridges (3 x 10-12), single-leg squats to a chair or step-downs from a low step, keeping the knee over the second toe (3 x 8-10 each side). Do them slowly and with good control. Combine them with short running drills where you focus on keeping the hips level.

## Side-to-side differences (asymmetry) are common
Source: Zifchock RA, Davis I, Higginson J, Royer T. The symmetry angle: a novel, robust method of quantifying asymmetry. Gait Posture 2008;27(4):622-627
Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=The+symmetry+angle%3A+a+novel%2C+robust+method+of+quantifying+asymmetry

Research on gait shows that most healthy runners have some difference between their left and right sides, so perfect symmetry is not normal or necessary. A side-to-side difference matters more when it is large, when it is getting bigger over time, when it follows an injury, or when it comes with pain on one side. In the app, "Stride symmetry" or "Side-to-side rhythm" compares the two sides; use it to track change over time, not as a diagnosis.

## Improving balance between the two legs
Source: Lauersen JB, Bertelsen DM, Andersen LB. The effectiveness of exercise interventions to prevent sports injuries: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials. Br J Sports Med 2014;48:871-877
Link: https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2013-092538

Single-leg exercises train each side on its own so the weaker side cannot hide: single-leg balance (30-45 seconds, progress to eyes closed or on a pillow), single-leg calf raises, step-ups, split squats and single-leg bridges. Start with the weaker side and match the reps on the stronger side. This meta-analysis found that strength training and balance (proprioception) training both lowered sports injury risk, so this work helps both symmetry and injury prevention.
