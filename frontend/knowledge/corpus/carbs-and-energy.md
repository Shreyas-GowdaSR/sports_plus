## Daily carbohydrate depends on how hard you train
Source: Thomas DT, Erdman KA, Burke LM. ACSM, AND and Dietitians of Canada joint position: Nutrition and Athletic Performance. Med Sci Sports Exerc 2016;48(3):543-568
Link: https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000000852

Carbohydrate (rice, chapati, idli, poha, fruit) is the main fuel for running. The joint position stand of the American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM), the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics and Dietitians of Canada sets daily carbohydrate by training load, in grams per kg of body weight:
- Light training or skill work: 3-5 g per kg per day.
- Moderate training, about 1 hour a day: 5-7 g per kg per day.
- Endurance training of 1-3 hours a day at moderate to high intensity: 6-10 g per kg per day.
- Very high load, more than 4-5 hours a day: 8-12 g per kg per day.
Carbohydrate should go up and down with the day's training, so a rest day needs less than a long-run day.

## Fat and total energy for athletes
Source: Thomas DT, Erdman KA, Burke LM. ACSM, AND and Dietitians of Canada joint position: Nutrition and Athletic Performance. Med Sci Sports Exerc 2016;48(3):543-568
Link: https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000000852

Athletes should get about 20-35% of their energy from fat. Going below 20% is not advised, because fat carries vitamins A, D, E and K and essential fatty acids. Energy needs change with body size, training load and the day's sessions. The first goal of an athlete's diet is to eat enough energy to cover training and normal body functions. Eating too little for long periods harms health and performance (see Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport).

## The meal before a run or race
Source: Thomas DT, Erdman KA, Burke LM. ACSM, AND and Dietitians of Canada joint position: Nutrition and Athletic Performance. Med Sci Sports Exerc 2016;48(3):543-568
Link: https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000000852

Before sessions longer than about an hour, a meal with 1-4 g carbohydrate per kg eaten 1-4 hours before helps top up the body's energy stores. Bigger meals need more time to settle. Choose foods that are low in fat, fibre and protein to avoid stomach problems: for example idli with a little chutney, plain dosa, poha, upma, bread with jam or banana, or curd rice. Always use foods you have practised with in training, not new foods on race day.

## Carbohydrate loading before a long race
Source: Burke LM, Hawley JA, Wong SHS, Jeukendrup AE. Carbohydrates for training and competition. J Sports Sci 2011;29(Suppl 1):S17-S27
Link: https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2011.585473

For races longer than about 90 minutes (half marathon and marathon), eating 10-12 g carbohydrate per kg per day for the 36-48 hours before the race fills the muscle energy stores (glycogen). Training is cut down (tapered) at the same time. In practice this means bigger portions of rice, chapati, idli, dosa, potatoes, fruit and sweet drinks, and less fibre and fat so the meals are easy to digest. For races shorter than 90 minutes, normal high-carbohydrate eating the day before is enough.

## Refuelling fast between two sessions
Source: Thomas DT, Erdman KA, Burke LM. ACSM, AND and Dietitians of Canada joint position: Nutrition and Athletic Performance. Med Sci Sports Exerc 2016;48(3):543-568
Link: https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000000852

When there are less than 8 hours between two hard sessions, eat about 1.0-1.2 g carbohydrate per kg per hour for the first 4 hours after the first session, in small frequent snacks. Adding protein helps muscle repair. When there is a full day before the next session, normal meals across the day refill energy stores and there is no rush.
