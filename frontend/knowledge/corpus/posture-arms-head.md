## Trunk position: a slight forward lean
Source: Teng HL, Powers CM. Influence of trunk posture on lower extremity energetics during running. Med Sci Sports Exerc 2015;47(3):625-630
Link: https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000000436

How much the upper body leans forward changes where the load goes. In this study, running with a little more forward trunk lean reduced the load on the knee (the kneecap joint) and moved more of the work to the hip muscles. Running very upright or leaning back puts more load on the knee. A good cue is to "run tall and lean slightly forward from the ankles, not from the waist", keeping the chest open. Bending forward at the waist is not the same thing and can strain the lower back.

## Why arm swing matters
Source: Arellano CJ, Kram R. The metabolic cost of human running: is swinging the arms worth it? J Exp Biol 2014;217:2456-2461
Link: https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.100420

Arm swing balances the twisting of the legs and saves energy. When runners in this study ran without swinging their arms, they used about 3% more energy with arms held behind the back, about 9% more with arms across the chest and about 13% more with hands on the head. So a free, relaxed arm swing is part of efficient running. Swing the arms forward and back from the shoulder with elbows bent at about 90 degrees, hands loose, and avoid crossing far over the middle of the body.

## Head, neck and gaze
Source: Barton CJ et al. Running retraining to treat lower limb injuries: a mixed-methods study of current evidence synthesised with expert opinion. Br J Sports Med 2016;50:513-526
Link: https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2015-095278

There is little direct research on head position, so this is based on common coaching and expert practice. The head should stay level and steady, eyes looking ahead about 10-20 metres, neck relaxed, jaw loose. Dropping the head to look at the feet or pushing the chin forward adds tension in the neck and shoulders and can pull the trunk out of position. Big side-to-side head movements usually come from the arms crossing the body or from tiredness, so fixing arm swing often fixes the head.

## Running economy: less bounce, less braking
Source: Folland JP, Allen SJ, Black MI, Handsaker JC, Forrester SE. Running technique is an important component of running economy and performance. Med Sci Sports Exerc 2017;49(7):1412-1423
Link: https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000001245

In a study of 97 runners, technique was clearly linked with how much energy runners used (running economy) and with their race performance. Bouncing up and down a lot (high vertical movement of the pelvis) and braking strongly at each landing were among the features linked with poorer economy. Practical cues: run "low and smooth", let the foot land closer under the body, and keep a quick, light rhythm rather than big bounding strides.
