## What REDs (under-fuelling) means
Source: Mountjoy M et al. 2023 International Olympic Committee's (IOC) consensus statement on Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (REDs). Br J Sports Med 2023;57:1073-1097
Link: https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2023-106994

Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (REDs) happens when an athlete eats too little energy for the training they do, for a long time. The body then has too little energy left for normal functions such as hormones, bones, immunity, and repair. It can happen by accident (not knowing how much training costs, a busy schedule, poor appetite after training) or on purpose (dieting to be lighter). It affects both female and male athletes of all levels.

## Warning signs of under-fuelling
Source: Mountjoy M et al. 2023 International Olympic Committee's (IOC) consensus statement on Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (REDs). Br J Sports Med 2023;57:1073-1097
Link: https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2023-106994

Signs that an athlete may be eating too little for their training include:
- Missed, irregular or stopped periods in women (this is not a normal effect of training).
- Low sex drive or low morning erections in men.
- Bone stress injuries or stress fractures, especially repeated ones.
- Getting ill often, feeling tired all the time, poor sleep, low mood or irritability.
- Performance getting worse despite training, and slow recovery.
Anyone with these signs should see a doctor or sports physician and a sports dietitian.

## Energy availability and how to avoid under-fuelling
Source: Thomas DT, Erdman KA, Burke LM. ACSM, AND and Dietitians of Canada joint position: Nutrition and Athletic Performance. Med Sci Sports Exerc 2016;48(3):543-568
Link: https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000000852

Energy availability is the energy left for the body after subtracting the energy used in training, expressed per kg of fat-free (lean) mass. About 45 kcal per kg of lean mass per day supports health. Earlier research used about 30 kcal per kg as a warning line, though the 2023 IOC statement says no single cut-off fits everyone. To avoid under-fuelling: eat more on heavy training days, do not skip meals around training, carry snacks on long days, and do not combine heavy training with strict dieting. Weight loss for sport should only be done slowly and with a sports dietitian.
