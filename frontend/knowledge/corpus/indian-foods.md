## Protein in common Indian foods
Source: Longvah T et al. Indian Food Composition Tables 2017. ICMR-National Institute of Nutrition, Hyderabad
Link: https://www.nin.res.in/
Cite: IFCT 2017 (ICMR-NIN)

Approximate protein in usual Indian servings, based on the Indian Food Composition Tables (IFCT 2017):
- 1 katori cooked dal, chana or rajma (from about 30 g dry): about 7 g.
- 1 glass (250 ml) milk: about 8 g. 1 katori curd: about 5 g.
- 50 g paneer: about 9 g. 100 g tofu: about 8-10 g.
- 25 g dry soya chunks (1 small katori cooked): about 13 g.
- 1 egg: about 6 g. 100 g cooked chicken: about 25 g. 100 g fish: about 20 g.
- 30 g peanuts or roasted chana: about 6-7 g. 1 katori sprouts: about 5 g.
- 1 chapati: about 3 g. 1 cup cooked rice: about 4 g. 2 idlis: about 4 g.
Cereals give some protein too, but on their own they are not enough for an athlete.

## Carbohydrate in common Indian foods
Source: Longvah T et al. Indian Food Composition Tables 2017. ICMR-National Institute of Nutrition, Hyderabad
Link: https://www.nin.res.in/
Cite: IFCT 2017 (ICMR-NIN)

Approximate carbohydrate in usual Indian servings (IFCT 2017 values with common portion sizes):
- 1 cup (about 150 g) cooked rice: about 45 g.
- 1 medium chapati: about 15 g. 1 plain dosa: about 20 g. 1 idli: about 8 g.
- 1 plate poha or upma: about 40 g.
- 1 banana: about 25-27 g. 3 dates: about 18 g. 1 medium sweet potato: about 25 g.
- 2 slices of bread: about 26 g. 1 tablespoon sugar or jaggery: about 12 g.
Whole grains and millets (brown rice, ragi, jowar, bajra) give more fibre and minerals; on the day before a race or just before a run, lower-fibre choices like white rice, idli and banana sit better in the stomach.

## A balanced Indian plate for an athlete
Source: ICMR-National Institute of Nutrition. Dietary Guidelines for Indians. Hyderabad, 2024
Link: https://www.nin.res.in/
Cite: ICMR-NIN 2024

The ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians (2024) recommend a varied plate: plenty of vegetables, green leafy vegetables and fruits; whole cereals and millets; pulses, beans, eggs, fish or meat; milk or curd; and a small amount of nuts, seeds and oil. They advise limiting ultra-processed foods high in fat, sugar and salt. For an athlete, the same plate works with bigger cereal portions on heavy training days and a protein food (dal, curd, paneer, egg, soya, chicken or fish) at every meal.

## Combining cereals and pulses for better protein
Source: Rogerson D. Vegan diets: practical advice for athletes and exercisers. J Int Soc Sports Nutr 2017;14:36
Link: https://doi.org/10.1186/s12970-017-0192-9

Cereals (rice, wheat, millets) are low in the amino acid lysine, and pulses (dal, chana, rajma) are low in methionine. Eating them together across the day gives a more complete protein. Traditional Indian combinations already do this: dal rice, rajma chawal, idli or dosa with sambar, khichdi, chapati with chana, and pesarattu. Adding curd or milk to a vegetarian meal improves protein quality further.

## Cheap protein for hostel, budget and travel
Source: Longvah T et al. Indian Food Composition Tables 2017. ICMR-National Institute of Nutrition, Hyderabad
Link: https://www.nin.res.in/
Cite: IFCT 2017 (ICMR-NIN)

Some of the cheapest protein sources per gram in India are soya chunks, eggs, peanuts, roasted chana, sprouts, dals, milk and curd. In a hostel mess, take an extra katori of dal, curd or the egg option, and keep roasted chana, peanuts or a packet of milk as snacks. While travelling, good options are curd rice, idli sambar, boiled eggs, roasted chana, peanut chikki, bananas and milk or buttermilk.

## Limit salt in normal meals, add it for long sweaty runs
Source: World Health Organization. Guideline: sodium intake for adults and children. Geneva: WHO, 2012
Link: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241504836
Cite: WHO 2012

The WHO advises adults to eat less than 5 g of salt (about 1 level teaspoon, or 2 g sodium) per day to protect the heart and blood pressure. Many Indian diets go above this through pickles, papad, namkeen and packaged snacks. Athletes who sweat a lot lose salt in sweat and may need some extra salt around long or hot training sessions, but the everyday diet should still avoid very salty processed foods.
