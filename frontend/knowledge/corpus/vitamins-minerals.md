## Anaemia is common in India and hurts running
Source: International Institute for Population Sciences. National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5), 2019-21: India fact sheet. Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, 2021
Link: https://rchiips.org/nfhs/
Cite: NFHS-5, 2019-21

According to the National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5, 2019-21), about 57% of Indian women aged 15-49 and about 25% of men are anaemic. Anaemia means too little haemoglobin to carry oxygen, and the most common cause is low iron. For runners it causes tiredness, breathlessness and poor performance. Female athletes, vegetarians and runners who train a lot are at higher risk. A simple blood test (haemoglobin and ferritin) is the way to know; iron tablets should only be taken on a doctor's advice.

## How to get more iron from Indian food
Source: Sim M et al. Iron considerations for the athlete: a narrative review. Eur J Appl Physiol 2019;119:1463-1478
Link: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00421-019-04157-y

Iron from meat, fish and eggs is absorbed better than iron from plant foods. Vegetarian sources include green leafy vegetables, rajma, chana, lentils, soya, bajra, jaggery, dates and seeds. Vitamin C (lemon, amla, guava, orange, tomato) eaten in the same meal helps absorption, while tea and coffee taken with or just after a meal reduce it, so keep tea about an hour away from meals. After hard exercise a hormone called hepcidin rises for about 3-6 hours and lowers iron absorption, so iron-rich meals work better in the morning or soon after training than many hours later.

## Calcium and bone health
Source: ICMR-National Institute of Nutrition. Nutrient Requirements for Indians: Recommended Dietary Allowances and Estimated Average Requirements. Hyderabad, 2020
Link: https://www.nin.res.in/
Cite: ICMR-NIN 2020

ICMR-NIN (2020) sets the calcium RDA for Indian adults at 1000 mg per day. Calcium, along with enough energy and vitamin D, is needed for strong bones, which matters for runners because of the repeated impact. Good sources are milk and curd (about 250-300 mg in a glass of milk), paneer, ragi (much higher in calcium than rice or wheat), sesame seeds (til), green leafy vegetables and small fish eaten with bones. Vegans can use ragi, tofu set with calcium, sesame and fortified foods.

## Vitamin D in athletes
Source: Owens DJ, Allison R, Close GL. Vitamin D and the athlete: current perspectives and new challenges. Sports Med 2018;48(Suppl 1):3-16
Link: https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-017-0841-9

Vitamin D helps the body use calcium and supports bones and muscles. Low vitamin D is common in athletes, especially those who train indoors, train very early or late, cover most of their skin, or have darker skin, so it is common even in sunny countries like India. Few foods contain much vitamin D (egg yolk, oily fish, fortified milk). Sensible sun exposure helps. Supplements should be based on a blood test and a doctor's advice, because very high doses can be harmful.

## Vitamin B12 for vegetarians and vegans
Source: Rogerson D. Vegan diets: practical advice for athletes and exercisers. J Int Soc Sports Nutr 2017;14:36
Link: https://doi.org/10.1186/s12970-017-0192-9

Vitamin B12 is found naturally almost only in animal foods. Lacto-vegetarians get some from milk, curd and paneer, while vegans get almost none and need fortified foods or a B12 supplement. Low B12 causes tiredness, anaemia, tingling in hands and feet and poor concentration. A blood test is worth doing if a vegetarian athlete feels tired all the time.
