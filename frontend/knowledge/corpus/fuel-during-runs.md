## How much carbohydrate to take per hour during a run
Source: Jeukendrup A. A step towards personalized sports nutrition: carbohydrate intake during exercise. Sports Med 2014;44(Suppl 1):S25-S33
Link: https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-014-0148-z

How much carbohydrate you need during exercise depends mainly on how long it lasts:
- Under about 45 minutes: no carbohydrate is needed; water is enough.
- About 45-75 minutes: small amounts, or even just rinsing the mouth with a sports drink, can help.
- 1-2 hours: up to about 30 g per hour.
- 2-3 hours: up to about 60 g per hour.
- More than about 2.5 hours: up to about 90 g per hour, but only using a mix of glucose and fructose and only if the gut has been trained to take it.
Start fuelling early in the run, in small regular amounts (for example every 15-20 minutes), rather than waiting until you feel tired.

## Indian foods and drinks for fuel during long runs
Source: Thomas DT, Erdman KA, Burke LM. ACSM, AND and Dietitians of Canada joint position: Nutrition and Athletic Performance. Med Sci Sports Exerc 2016;48(3):543-568
Link: https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000000852

The ACSM position stand says carbohydrate during exercise can come from drinks, gels, chews or normal foods, as long as the athlete tolerates them. Cheap Indian options with about 25-30 g carbohydrate each: 1 ripe banana; 3-4 dates; 500 ml of homemade drink with 2 tablespoons of sugar or glucose powder and a pinch of salt; 2 tablespoons of raisins; a small boiled sweet potato. On hot days or runs longer than 90 minutes, a drink with some salt (or ORS) is better than plain water.

## Training the gut and avoiding stomach problems
Source: Jeukendrup AE. Training the gut for athletes. Sports Med 2017;47(Suppl 1):101-110
Link: https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-017-0690-6

Stomach cramps, bloating, nausea and running to the toilet are common in distance runners. The gut can be trained: practising your race-day food and drink during long training runs improves how well carbohydrate is absorbed and reduces stomach problems. Meals high in fibre, fat or protein just before running raise the risk, as does dehydration. Never try a new gel, drink or food for the first time on race day.

## Race morning breakfast
Source: Burke LM, Hawley JA, Wong SHS, Jeukendrup AE. Carbohydrates for training and competition. J Sports Sci 2011;29(Suppl 1):S17-S27
Link: https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2011.585473

A carbohydrate-rich meal 1-4 hours before a race tops up the liver's energy store after the overnight fast. For early-morning races (5-6 am starts) most runners eat a small, familiar, low-fibre snack 1-2 hours before: 2-3 idlis, a banana with 2 slices of bread and jam, or a bowl of poha, with a glass of water. Go lighter if you are nervous or the start is very early. A practised carbohydrate drink is an option for runners who cannot eat solid food early.
